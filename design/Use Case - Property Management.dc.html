Where a managing agent puts its channels

A channel is a group of people plus the work they share: the conversation, the jobs, the calendar and the wiki. Most agents run one per building, split by who is allowed to see what.

One channel per building

Meadowridge in one place: the trustees, the residents, your site staff. Owners and trustees join as guests, free, and see only their own building.

Maintenance

Where a resident reports a burst geyser and it becomes a numbered job with a contractor and a clock: instead of a voicenote to somebody’s personal phone.

Trustees

Quotes, approvals and the meeting pack. The decision and the three quotes it was made on stay together, which is what the next AGM will ask about.

Residents

Notices, outages and the questions everyone asks. One announcement replaces forty phone calls to the office.

Levies & arrears

Collections and the policy behind them. Sensitive, so it stays out of the residents channel and in front of the people who decide.

Agency

Your own portfolio. Rota, contractors, and the honest view of a building before the trustees are in the room.

Each channel has a seat count on your plan: 30 on Starter, 100 on Pro, unlimited on Max, shared between your staff and your guests. A residents channel for an eighty-unit scheme needs Pro; a trustees channel fits inside Starter. Compare plans.

Every job has a number, an owner and a clock

Emergencies, resident queries, quote approvals, compliance and arrears as tracked items. When a trustee asks what happened to the lift, the answer is a record rather than a recollection.

Categories that match the portfolio

Emergency, resident query, quote approval, compliance, arrears. Each routed to the person who handles it, not to whoever answered the phone.

Response times per category

An emergency has minutes, a parking dispute has days. Trustees see the same clock you do, which ends the argument about responsiveness.

A report becomes a job

The resident describes it in the channel with photographs; one step turns it into a job with the contractor assigned and the images attached.

Two in the morning is not the time to guess your mandate

The procedure lives on the building’s wiki: what counts as an emergency, what you may spend before phoning a trustee, and what has to be photographed before anyone touches it.

1

Contain it before you cost it

Close the main, isolate the board, get people out of the lift. The invoice conversation happens afterwards and is always cheaper than the delay.

2

Photograph everything, immediately

Both units, both sides of the damage, the failed part. No insurer has ever accepted a claim on a description alone.

3

Spend to the mandate, then ask

Up to the agreed limit you proceed and report. Above it you phone the trustee on duty and record their answer in the channel, at the time it was given.

Your supervisor is on a roof, not at a desk
