Kolabr vs Zendesk

A helpdesk is built for volume from people you will never meet. Kolabr is built for the forty clients whose names you know, where the request is one moment in a relationship that has run for years. Same discipline of owners, categories and clocks, without a portal standing between you and the person asking.

Jump to the comparison See pricing

The short version

If your customers are strangers and there are thousands of them, buy a helpdesk. If they are named accounts you will still have in five years, the queue is the wrong shape and a channel is the right one.

Proof takes one week

Take one client off the portal and give them a channel. By Friday you will see requests arriving from people who never bothered to log in, and answers coming from colleagues who never had a seat.

A queue is the wrong shape for forty named clients

Helpdesk software is designed around a stranger with a problem. It is why there is a portal, a form and a set of macros: when the volume is thousands a month from people you will never meet again, structure at the front door is what keeps it survivable. That design is a poor fit when the person raising the request is the same practice manager you spoke to yesterday and will speak to every week for the next three years.

In Kolabr the request sits inside the relationship rather than in front of it. The client is already in the channel, so they type, and one step turns that message into a numbered request with a category, an owner and a response target. The thread is attached, the history is right there, and nobody had to log in to a second system to tell you something.

Per-agent pricing decides who is allowed to help

When every person who can reply costs a full seat, the engineer who actually knows the answer gets left out and the request is relayed to them instead. The relay is where context is lost, and where a two-minute answer becomes a day and a half.

Kolabr charges for the people on your plan and then stops counting. Put the engineer, the account manager and the person who wrote the thing in the channel, because none of them costs extra. The client and their whole team are guests, free, on every plan.

What Kolabr does not try to be

This is the comparison where scope matters most, so it is worth being plain about it.

A contact centre

No IVR, call queues, skills-based routing or workforce management. If you run phone support at scale, you need software built for it.

A public help centre

The wiki is for the people in the channel, not a published site for the general public to search.

A deflection engine

Kolabr is not trying to stop your clients contacting you. With named accounts that is usually the wrong goal anyway.

Thousands of tickets a month

Kolabr suits tens of clients with real relationships, not anonymous volume from the general public.

What each one actually costs you

Zendesk is priced per agent on annual terms: Support Team at $19 for ticketing only, Suite Team at $55 and Suite Professional at $115, with Enterprise quoted. Copilot and the other bundles are charged per agent on top, and AI agents are billed per automated resolution. Kolabr is $9.99, $19.99 or $39.99 per user per month, with nothing stacked on it.

The difference compounds with how many of your own people are involved. A ten-person firm where everybody occasionally answers a client pays for ten people in Kolabr. On per-agent pricing the same firm either buys ten seats at contact-centre rates or decides that six of them are not allowed to reply. What your plan buys is seats per channel: 30 on Starter, 100 on Pro, unlimited on Max, shared between your people and your guests.

Also worth checking: whether somebody who answers both customer and internal requests needs a seat on two separate products. Compare Kolabr plans.

Questions people ask before moving

Is Kolabr a helpdesk?

No, and it is the comparison we are most careful about. A helpdesk is built for volume from people you will never meet: a portal, a queue, macros, deflection. Kolabr is built for named relationships that last years, where the client is in the channel with you and the request is one part of an ongoing conversation. If your support is thousands of tickets a month from strangers, a helpdesk is the right tool.

Why would a support team choose Kolabr instead?

Because not all support looks like a contact centre. A managed service provider with forty clients, a firm with named accounts, a supplier with a handful of large customers: in those, the people raising requests are the same faces every week, and putting a portal between you and them removes the context rather than organising it.

Do we pay per agent?

No. Kolabr is priced per person on your plan, and anyone in your company can be in a channel and answer a request. Nobody has to decide whether a colleague is worth an agent seat before letting them help, and the client and their staff are guests at no cost.

Do clients need to log into a portal?

No. They are in the channel, which is where they already talk to you. There is no separate system to learn, no password to forget and no moment where somebody gives up and sends an email instead.

Can we run both?

Yes, and some do. A helpdesk handles high-volume inbound from the general public while Kolabr runs the named accounts, where the relationship matters more than the queue. The two rarely overlap in practice, because they are shaped for different kinds of customer.

What does Kolabr include that a helpdesk plan charges for?

Response-time targets on every plan rather than a higher tier, a wiki in every channel instead of a help centre you build and publish, full chat with direct messages, video calls, and scheduled events. One price, with no per-agent add-ons stacked on it.
