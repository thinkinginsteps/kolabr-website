Where a practice puts its channels

A channel is a group of people plus the work they share: the conversation, the RFIs and variations, the calendar and the wiki. Most practices land on one channel per project, and a few standing ones.

Sable Bay Precinct

The project channel. Client, contractor, engineer, QS and landscape architect in one place, each of them a guest, none of them paying for a seat.

Design & drawings

Where revisions are issued and RFIs are answered. The transmittal, the marked-up detail and the answer sit together instead of in three inboxes.

Client review

Sign-offs and sample approvals. When the client approves, it is approved in writing, with a date, against the sheet it concerns.

Site queries

The site agent photographs the clash at 07:10, and the answer lands before the trade moves on. No “I sent it to someone at your office”.

Council submission

Comments, responses and the resubmission checklist for one authority, kept out of the design conversation.

Studio

Your own team only. Resourcing, fee queries, internal critique: the things a client should never scroll past.

Each channel has a seat count on your plan: 30 on Starter, 100 on Pro, unlimited on Max, shared between your studio and your guests. A project with a client, a contractor and four consultants sits comfortably inside a Starter channel. Compare plans.

Every RFI has an owner and a due date

A question from site becomes a numbered request in the channel it belongs to: categorised as an RFI, a variation, a site instruction or a submission, owned by a name, answered against a drawing. Six months later you can still show who asked, who answered and when.

Your own categories

RFI, drawing revision, variation, site instruction, submission. Each one routes to the person who actually answers it.

A clock the contractor can see

An SLA per category, so “we are looking into it” becomes two working days: visible to the client, which is the point.

A message becomes an RFI

The site agent writes it as a message; anyone in the studio turns that message into a numbered RFI in one step, photographs attached.

Nobody prices off a superseded sheet again

Issue the revision in the channel and the contractor, the QS and the engineer all see the same transmittal at the same moment. The superseded list is published with it.

The revision knows why it exists

Link the new revision to the RFI or instruction that caused it. In a year, when someone asks why the sheet moved, the answer is on the record instead of in a memory.

2

One issue, one list, one place

Transmittal, drawing register and revision notes live on the channel wiki. Guests read them without a licence, so there is no “please resend the latest set”.

3

Cost changes go past the principal

A variation cannot be approved by a quiet email. It is a request with an owner, a price and a signature, sitting against the sheet it changes.

The answer reaches site before the trade moves on

Your technologist is at a desk with the model open. The site agent is standing in front of the clash with a phone. Same channel, same RFI number, same photographs, and the answer is written down where the contractor has to acknowledge it.

Photographs, not descriptions

Shot at the clash, attached to the request, visible to the engineer in seconds.

A call from the request

Start a video call and share the detail on screen when writing it out would take longer.

Works on a bad line

The site is a slab with one bar of signal. The app is built for that, not for the office.

No personal numbers

Nobody hands out a mobile number to a contractor they will meet again on the next job.

Submission dates, site meetings and the tender clock

Design reviews, consultant coordination, the monthly site meeting, the council resubmission, the date tender pack B must be out. Scheduled in the channel it concerns, synced to Outlook and Google, and tied to the request it came from.

Nothing hides between projects

Which schemes are waiting on you, which are waiting on the client, and which RFI is quietly turning into a delay claim.

One view across every project

Open RFIs per project, what is overdue, which client has been sitting on an approval for four days. The whole practice on one dashboard instead of chasing six people for a status.
