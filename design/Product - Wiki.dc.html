The answers, written down where the work happens

Each channel has its own wiki: the runbooks your team follows, the checklists a client keeps asking for, the reasons behind decisions taken months ago. Sectioned, searchable and reviewed on a date, not whenever someone remembers.

Writing one takes a minute

A title, a one-line summary and the body. Tags, category and keywords are there when you want the article to be found in more than one way.

1

Draft, then publish

Save it half-written and come back. Nothing is visible to the channel until you say so, and a published article can go back to draft.

2

Written like a document

Headings, lists, quotes, code blocks, links, images and tables. Attach the PDF or the CSV people actually need beside it.

3

Tagged so it surfaces

Category, tags and alternative search terms decide where it shows up, including as a suggestion while someone is raising a request.

It answers the question before it becomes a request

Published articles are offered as suggestions while a person is writing a request: client or colleague. The ones that answer the question end the thread before your team ever sees it.

Keeping it worth reading

A wiki is only useful if someone owns it. See how requests feed it.

Sections, not a folder of files

Group articles the way the channel thinks: Getting started, one section per service line, Onboarding, Runbooks. New members read the first section and are useful by lunchtime.

Is this a documentation tool?

Not the kind you migrate a company into. It is the wiki for one channel, read by the people in that channel, which is why it stays short enough to be true.

One wiki per channel

No company-wide tree nobody prunes. Each channel keeps the pages its own work needs, and they are the pages its members see.

Guests read it too

Publish the articles a client should have, the checklist, the cut-off times, the escalation ladder, and stop sending them as attachments.

Included in the plan

The wiki comes with the channel. No separate knowledge-base subscription, and no per-reader charge.
