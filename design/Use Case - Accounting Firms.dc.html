Where a practice puts its channels

A channel is a group of people plus the work they share: the conversation, the requests, the deadline calendar and the wiki. Most firms run one per client, split by service line.

One channel per client

Harlow Foods in one place: their financial director, their creditors clerk, their MD. Guests, free, seeing only their own affairs.

Monthly bookkeeping

Reconciliations, queries and the management accounts pack. The place a bookkeeper asks about an unidentified deposit instead of guessing.

Year-end & audit

Fieldwork, records requests and the queries that hold up a sign-off. Every document request with a date and a name on it.

Payroll

Monthly runs, variances and EMP501. Separate, because payroll questions are urgent and personal and should not sit behind an audit thread.

Tax & SARS

Returns, verifications, assessments. When a letter arrives with twenty-one days on it, everyone can see the clock.

Practice

Your own people. Capacity, deadline load, and the honest conversation about a client who never sends anything on time.

Each channel has a seat count on your plan: 30 on Starter, 100 on Pro, unlimited on Max, shared between your staff and your client’s. A client with three finance people and your team of five sits comfortably inside Starter. Compare plans.

Nothing waits on a document nobody asked for twice

Filings, queries, audit items and bookkeeping tasks as numbered items with an owner and a due date. What is outstanding from the client is a list, not a feeling, and the client can see it too.

Categories that match the work

Filing, query, audit, bookkeeping, payroll. Each routes to the person who does it, not to whoever the client emailed.

Statutory dates with a clock

VAT201, EMP201, provisional tax, CIPC. The deadline is on the item, and “waiting on client” is visible to the client.

A message becomes a request

The client mentions a SARS letter in the channel; one step turns it into a tracked item with the document list attached.

Every junior handles a SARS letter the way you would

The procedure sits on the channel wiki beside the client it concerns: what kind of letter it is, what to ask for, and who reviews before anything is submitted.

1

Diarise the deadline the day it arrives

Not the day you start. Twenty-one days sounds generous until the client takes twelve of them finding one invoice.

2

Ask for everything at once

One consolidated list in the channel, not four messages across a fortnight. Every round trip is two days you do not have.

3

A partner reviews, then post the reference

What you submit becomes part of the record. Post the submission reference back in the channel so nobody reconstructs it in eight months.

February does not happen at a desk

Year-end runs on site visits, stock counts and a partner answering a client between two meetings. The channel goes with them, and what they agree lands on the file, not in a text message.

Answer from anywhere

A partner unblocks a junior in thirty seconds, and it is recorded against the client.

Documents at the stock count

Photograph the sheet at the warehouse rather than promising to scan it later.

Notifications per client

The client filing tomorrow interrupts you. The one on a quarterly cycle does not.

No personal WhatsApp

Client records stop living on a phone that leaves when a manager does.

The compliance calendar, per client, in one place

VAT on the 25th, EMP201 on the 7th, provisional tax, the audit sign-off meeting and the management accounts pack. In the channel it belongs to, synced to Outlook and Google.

Which deadlines are at risk, and whose fault that is

Filings due, documents outstanding from clients, and the engagement quietly consuming three times its fee in chasing.

Every client on one screen

Deadlines, outstanding documents and how long each client has been sitting on a request. The fee conversation goes better with a list.
