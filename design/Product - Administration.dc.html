Run it all from one place

Channels, people, roles, SLA profiles, routing and playbooks: the settings that decide how work reaches your team, in one admin area rather than scattered through each channel.

Who is in, and what they can do

One list of everyone with access, your staff and the customers you invited, with the channel they belong to, their role and whether the account is still active.

1

Staff and customers, one view

Filter to staff or customers, search any column, export the lot. You can see at a glance who is a guest and which channel they were invited into.

2

Roles, not guesswork

Admin, agent or participant: set per person, applied per channel. Changing someone’s role takes effect everywhere they are a member.

3

Disable without deleting

Someone leaves, an account goes quiet: switch it off and the history stays intact, including everything they wrote in the channel.

Set the rules once

Categories, SLA profiles, routing, rules, playbooks, custom fields and service notices: the configuration behind every request, on tabs instead of buried in menus.

Everything that happened, with a name on it

Logins, role changes, members added, requests reassigned, every email the system sent, with the user, the entity, the ID and the IP. Retained for 24 months and exportable as CSV.

The controls that matter as you grow

Enough control to run a real operation, without a week of setup. See what it does to requests.

Is this a lot to set up?

No. The defaults work on day one: create a channel, invite people, start. The rest is there when your operation is big enough to need it.

Start with the defaults

One SLA profile, one category set, no routing rules. Add a profile the first time a client needs a tighter promise than the rest.

Guests stay out of it

Administration is yours. The people you invite see their channels and their requests, and nothing about how the place is configured.

Answers for auditors

Who had access, who changed what, when a request was reassigned. Exportable, so the annual review takes an afternoon rather than a fortnight.
