Kolabr vs Microsoft Teams

Teams was built for the people inside your company. Kolabr runs your internal channels too, and every client, contractor, consultant and supplier in the same place, on the same terms, without a second tool or a second bill. This page sets the two side by side on the things that decide it: what an outsider costs you, how quickly you can get them into a room, and whether anyone can tell what was promised.

The short version

Kolabr handles the internal room as readily as the client one: your own team channels, your own private and group chats. The difference shows the moment someone outside the company has to be in the conversation, which in most businesses is most days. Kolabr makes it easy and frictionless.

Proof takes one week

Give your most demanding client a channel and invite them. By Friday you will see it: they answer in the channel instead of the phone, every promise has an owner and a date, and nobody has asked you to resend anything. That is the week that decides it.

The difference is what happens when someone is not on your payroll

In Teams, there are three different ways to bring in someone from outside, and you have to pick one before you start. One lets you chat to them but nothing more. One adds them to a team and creates an account for them in your company directory. One links your organisation to theirs, if their IT agrees to set it up too. Each is configured in a different place, and each has its own rules about what the person can see.

In Kolabr there is one mechanism. You invite an email address into a channel and that person is a member of that channel: able to read the wiki, raise a request, join a call, see the dates. They are never billed, they never appear in a directory you have to govern, and they cannot see any channel they were not invited to. That is the whole model, and it is why a firm with eleven staff can run two hundred client relationships without an administrator.

Chat is not a system of record

Both products are good at conversation. Neither conversation, on its own, tells you what was promised, to whom, by when. Teams answers that by adding something alongside it: Planner, Lists, or a helpdesk product. That means a second tool, a second place to look and a second thing to keep tidy. Kolabr answers it in the same channel: any message becomes a numbered request with a category, an owner, a due date and a response-time target the client can see.

One place, not three

The conversation, the request it produced and the article explaining how to handle it are in the same channel. No tab hunting, and nothing to license separately.

The client sees the clock

Response targets are visible to the people waiting. Uncomfortable at first, and then it is the reason they stop phoning to ask.

A view across every channel

What is open, overdue or unassigned across all your clients at once, rather than opening eleven teams in turn.

What Kolabr does not try to be

Worth knowing before you move, so nothing surprises you in month three. In every case below, the answer is to keep what you have and put the client-facing work in Kolabr: the two sit alongside each other without argument.

A phone system

Kolabr has video and voice calls inside a channel, started from the request they concern. It is not a PBX and does not replace Teams Phone or your calling plans.

An Office suite

Documents, spreadsheets and mail stay where they are. Kolabr shares files in a channel and keeps them with the work; it does not try to own your document estate.

A reason to rip anything out

Kolabr runs internal channels perfectly well, so plenty of teams do move everything. But nothing forces the decision at once, start with one client channel and let the internal side follow when it suits you.

An AI layer across your mailbox

Copilot reaches across mail, documents and meetings. Kolabr does not compete with that layer and stays out of the way of whatever you have chosen there.

Questions people ask before moving

Can Microsoft Teams work with clients outside my company?

Yes. Microsoft offers three routes: external access (federation) for chat and calls with people in other Microsoft 365 domains, guest access to add someone into a team properly, and shared channels built on B2B direct connect. Each has a different security model and a different place to configure it, so the practical question is less whether it can and more how much administration stands between you and the first invitation. In Kolabr that step is an email address.

Do Microsoft Teams guests need a licence?

Not an extra Microsoft 365 licence. A guest does become a real identity in your directory, covered by Microsoft Entra External ID and its billing model, and counted in your tenant’s governance and access reviews. In Kolabr a guest is simply a person in a channel, never billed, never in a directory, and nothing to review later.

Is Kolabr cheaper than Microsoft Teams?

It depends on how many outside people you work with. Teams is priced per internal seat from $4, Kolabr from $9.99, but Kolabr never charges for a guest and needs nothing underneath it, so a ten-person firm working with two hundred clients pays for ten people. Once you add Teams Phone, Teams Premium or Copilot, the per-seat gap narrows or reverses. And if you already hold Microsoft 365 for mail and Office, cost is not the question worth asking: external collaboration is.

Can we use Kolabr and Microsoft Teams together?

Many do at first, and some stay that way. The usual path is to start with one client channel in Kolabr while Teams keeps whatever is wired into Office, SharePoint and Exchange, then move internal channels across as people ask for them, since Kolabr handles those too. Calendar sync means scheduled events land in Outlook throughout, so nobody ends up with two diaries.

What does Kolabr do that Teams does not?

Three things principally. Requests with a category, an owner and a response-time target, visible to the client. A wiki that sits beside the conversation in the same channel and that guests can read. And a channel boundary that means a client sees one channel and nothing else, without a tenant-level configuration exercise first.

Which one should we choose?

Ask which conversations currently go wrong. Kolabr runs your internal channels and your client channels in one place, with guests who are free and instant, requests carrying owners and deadlines, and a wiki beside the work. Teams is the stronger fit if what you mainly need is telephony and an AI layer across your mailbox. For most firms whose work involves people outside the company, the daily friction is the part Kolabr is built to remove.
