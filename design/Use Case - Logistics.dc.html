Where a carrier puts its channels

A channel is a group of people plus the work they share: the conversation, the exceptions, the calendar and the wiki. Most carriers run one per account, split by what the client needs to see.

One channel per account

Cold Harvest in one place: their supply chain manager, their depot receiving, their head of logistics. Guests, free, seeing only their own freight.

Loads in transit

The live picture. Where a controller posts a delay before the client’s depot works it out for themselves.

Exceptions & delays

Cold chain, breakdowns, borders. The channel a client will forgive you for using, and never forgive you for not using.

Claims & PODs

Proof of delivery, shorts and damages. Six outstanding PODs is six invoices you cannot send, visible to the people who can find them.

Bookings & planning

Slots, lanes, rates. Next week’s plan agreed in writing rather than on a Friday phone call.

Control room

Your own staff. Shift handover, fleet and the frank assessment of a trailer before a client hears about it.

Each channel has a seat count on your plan: 30 on Starter, 100 on Pro, unlimited on Max, shared between your controllers and the client’s depots. A national account with six receiving sites needs Pro; a single-lane customer fits in Starter. Compare plans.

Exceptions with a number, an owner and a clock

Cold chain events, delays, claims, PODs and bookings as tracked items. When a client asks what happened to a load in July, the answer is a record with a temperature log attached.

Categories that match freight

Cold chain, delay, claim, POD, booking. Each routed to the controller, the supervisor or compliance, not to whoever picked up.

A clock both sides can see

Fifteen minutes on a cold chain report, six days on a POD. The client watches the same timer, which is worth more than a monthly service report.

An alarm becomes an exception

The controller posts it in the channel; one step makes it a numbered exception with the reefer download attached and the client already looking at it.

Every controller works an excursion the same way

The procedure lives on the account’s wiki: what the thresholds are, who to tell in what order, and what evidence to pull while the trailer is still moving.

1

Verify, then escalate, in that order

Check the second probe. A probe fault reported as an excursion costs you a load; a real excursion reported late costs you the account.

2

Recover while you report

Backup unit, technician, nearest cold store. Do not wait for a decision to start moving: the clock on the product runs either way.

3

Tell the client within fifteen minutes

Before their depot notices. What happened, what is on the trailer, what you are doing and when it will arrive, with the log attached.

Nights, depots and the side of the N1

A supervisor on a forecourt at two in the morning, a controller on the night shift, a depot receiving clerk with a tally sheet and a phone. All three are on the same exception, and none of them is typing it up afterwards.
