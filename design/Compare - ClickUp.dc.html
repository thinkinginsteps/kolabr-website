Kolabr vs ClickUp

ClickUp tracks your team’s tasks. Kolabr runs the relationship those tasks came from: a channel per client with the conversation, the requests and the wiki in one place, and guests who can actually take part without becoming a line on your invoice.

Jump to the comparison See pricing

The short version

A client who can only watch is not collaborating. In Kolabr every guest can post, raise a request, read the wiki and join a call, on every plan, at no cost.

Proof takes one week

Open a channel for one client and invite everyone on their side who has ever had a question. Nothing to configure, no seat arithmetic, and by Friday the requests arrive from the people who actually have them.

A guest who can only look is not much of a guest

ClickUp’s free guests are view-only. The moment a client needs to comment, approve or change anything, they take a guest seat from an allowance that scales with how many paid users you have, and once that allowance is gone the next guest is billed as a full member. There is a further rule that catches people out: a guest whose email domain matches your workspace is converted to a paid member automatically.

Kolabr has one kind of guest and they participate fully. They raise requests, answer questions, read the wiki and join calls, and they are free on every plan. Nothing converts, nothing is metered, and nobody on your side has to think about who is worth a seat before inviting the person who has the answer.

Tasks live inside the request, and die with it

Kolabr has tasks. What it does not have is a task manager. Every task belongs to the request that created it, is assigned to somebody, and disappears when that request is closed. There is no separate board to groom and no second place to look for what is outstanding, because what is outstanding for a client is the list of open requests.

This is a deliberate limit rather than a missing feature. Free-standing lists are easy to create and hard to keep honest, and a year in they describe a version of the work that stopped being true months ago. Tying every task to a request means the list is exactly as current as the request it belongs to.

One home. Open requests are the answer to what is outstanding. There is no second answer.
No orphans. A task cannot exist without a request, so it cannot exist without a reason.
Nothing to tidy. Close the request and its tasks go with it. No backlog grooming ritual.

Nothing to configure before you can use it

Every channel has the same shape, so the setup is a name and an invitation.

No hierarchy to invent

No spaces inside folders inside lists. A channel holds a relationship, and requests live in it.

No custom-field project

What is open, overdue or unassigned across every client is a view you open, not a dashboard somebody builds.

One plan for everyone

What Kolabr does not try to be

Worth knowing before you move, so nothing surprises you in month three.

A planning tool

No sprints, dependency chains, workload charts or Gantt views. If you plan delivery that way, keep the tool that does it.

A standalone task manager

Tasks exist inside requests and nowhere else. That is the design, not a gap waiting to be filled.

An automation platform

Routing, SLAs and playbooks are there, but Kolabr is not a builder for arbitrary workflows.

A time-tracking system

Requests record what was asked and when it was answered. They do not replace whatever you bill from.

Questions people ask before moving

Are ClickUp guests free?

A view-only guest is free and unlimited. A guest who can comment or edit takes a guest seat from an allowance that scales with your paid users, and once that allowance runs out the next one is charged as a full member. There is also a rule worth knowing: a guest whose email domain matches your workspace is converted to a paid member automatically. In Kolabr a guest can post, raise requests and read the wiki, and is free on every plan.

Is Kolabr a project management tool?

No. Kolabr organises work by relationship rather than by project: a channel per client, site or department, with requests inside it that carry owners, categories and response targets. If you need sprints, dependencies and workload charts, keep a planning tool for that and run the client side in Kolabr.

Does Kolabr have tasks?

Yes, inside a request. A request carries its own checklist, assigned and ticked off as the work moves, and those tasks close when the request closes. There is no free-standing task manager, because a list that floats loose from the thing that caused it is how a tool fills up with work nobody remembers agreeing to.

Which is cheaper?

ClickUp Unlimited at $7 and Business at $12 per user per month are below Kolabr per internal seat. The number that moves is participating guests: if your clients need to comment, approve or raise anything, they consume guest seats and eventually become paid members, and in Kolabr they never do.

We have built a lot in ClickUp. Is that wasted?

Not at all, and it is a reason to keep it for internal delivery. The usual approach is to leave the build where it is and open Kolabr channels for the client-facing side, where the conversation, the requests and the wiki need to be somewhere a client can be without a seat or a licence.

What does Kolabr do that ClickUp does not?

Full chat with threads and direct messages in the same place as the work, requests with response targets a client can see, a wiki in every channel, and guests who participate without ever becoming billable. And it works the day you open it, because there is nothing to configure first.
